# Entanglement experiments explained (Exp 1 and Exp 2), in plain words

Last updated: 2026-10-30. All numbers are means over 3 seeds and come from the run records (`records/results/all_runs.csv`,
generated by `outputs/write_entangle_explained.py`). The tables here show the methods WITHOUT the Weight Saliency mask; the versions
with the mask are in `records/results/entanglement_experiments.md` (they behave almost the same, differences are listed there).
The configurations are each method's best configuration (`records/results/method_comparison.md`): S1 - Gradient Ascent G3,
GA + Descent D1, Teacher-Student T1, Random Relabeling R1, Boundary Relabeling C1-mix; S2 - G3-S2, D1-S2, T4-best S2, R1-S2, C0.

## 0. The basic idea: what "entanglement" means and how the three parts are made

**Question we want to answer:** are some forget images harder to unlearn than others, and does it depend on how similar ("entangled")
a forget image is to the images we keep (the retain set)?

**How each forget image gets an entanglement score:**
1. We pass every training image through the Original model and take its 512-number feature vector (the layer just before the classifier).
2. For one forget image, we look for the 50 retain images whose feature vectors are closest to it, and take the average distance to them.
3. A **small distance** means the forget image looks like many retain images -> **highly entangled**.
   A **large distance** means it looks unlike the retain images (unusual, distinctive) -> **low entanglement**.

**How the forget set is split:** all forget images are sorted by this distance and cut into three equal parts:
- **low part** = the third that is farthest from the retain images (least entangled),
- **medium part** = the middle third,
- **high part** = the third that is closest to the retain images (most entangled).

Part sizes: S1 (4500 airplanes) -> 1500 images per part; S2-10 -> 450; S2-30 -> 1350; S2-50 -> 2250.

**What we measure in every run** (all on the training images of the run's own forget set, plus the full test set):
forget acc (how many forget images the model still classifies correctly), retain acc, test acc, MIA acc (how well an attacker can tell
the forget images from unseen test images of the same class; 50% = cannot tell), and **avg_gap** = the average absolute difference
between the unlearned model and the matching Retrain model on these four numbers. **avg_gap 0 = behaves exactly like retraining;
lower is better.** For the Original model (no unlearning at all) avg_gap tells how far the model is from the Retrain model before
anything is done - it is the amount of work an unlearning method has to do.

## 1. Experiment 1 - each part unlearned on its own

The aim: unlearn each part separately in the same way and check whether the forget accuracy after unlearning rises from the low part
to the high part (= highly entangled images are harder to forget), while retain accuracy stays high.

### 1.1 What runs - S1 (single-class forgetting), corrected setup (2026-10-30)
For every seed (3) and every part (low, medium, high):
- **Forget set = that part (1500 airplanes). Retain set = the normal S1 retain set (all non-airplane training images).** The other two
  parts (3000 airplanes) are **not used at all** during unlearning (neither forgotten nor kept); after unlearning we only measure how many
  of them the model still recognises ("excluded acc").
- **Reference:** the normal S1 Retrain model (trained without any airplane) evaluated on that part. It recognises 0% of every part, so
  the target is forget acc 0% with retain acc 100% and test acc 85.4% (the Retrain test accuracy without the airplane class).
- **The Original model** evaluated on that part (no unlearning): 100% forget acc, avg_gap 28.6-29.5.
- **Every method** (5 methods x without / with the Weight Saliency mask) unlearns that part starting from the Original model, with its
  best S1 configuration: 3 seeds x 3 parts x 10 = **90 unlearning runs** + 9 Original and 9 Retrain evaluations. No new Retrain training
  is needed.

(Our first S1 run kept the other two parts in the retain set. That made S1 a subset-forgetting problem - the retained airplanes keep the
class alive - so it did not test what we wanted and has been replaced by this setup.)

- **Gradient Ascent (G3):** forget acc 0.4% / 0.4% / 0.7%; retain acc 98.8-99.5%, test acc 82.7-83.9%; attack accuracy 49.6-51.4% (target

- **Teacher-Student (T1):** forget acc 0.0% / 0.0% / 0.1%, retain 100%, test 84.8%; attack 52.4-54.2%. Complete forgetting on every part.
- **Random Relabeling (R1, with its retain term as in the paper):** forget acc 2.8% / 0.6% / 0.5%, retain 100%. The only method with a

- **Boundary Relabeling (C1-mix):** forget acc 0% / 0% / 0% and 0% on the unused airplanes; retain 100%; attack 50.3-53.1%.

  spreads to the whole class (unused airplanes 0-5.4%). Forget accuracy does **not** rise from low to high, so at the accuracy level the
  hypothesis "highly entangled images are harder to forget" is not supported for class forgetting. Entanglement shows only weakly: the
  high run leaves slightly more of the other airplanes recognised (Gradient Ascent 5.4%, Random Relabeling 2.2%), and GA + Descent's
  attack accuracy is highest on the high part.

### 1.3 What runs - S2 (subset forgetting), RUM protocol
For every S2 scenario (S2-10, S2-30, S2-50), seed (3) and part:
1. **A new Retrain model** trained from scratch for 182 epochs on all training images except that part (the other two parts stay in its
   training data): 3 x 3 x 3 = **27 Retrain models**.
2. **The Original model evaluated on that part** (27 evaluations).
3. **Every method** (10 variants) unlearns only that part starting from the Original; the other two parts are treated as retain images
   (in subset forgetting the rest of the cats / dogs / trucks is retained anyway): **270 unlearning runs**.

### 1.4 What the Retrain models do in S2
The other 90/70/50% of the cats, dogs and trucks stay in training in every case. The Retrain models (S2-30; S2-10 and S2-50 are
almost the same) recognise **79.0%** of the low part, **94.7%** of the medium part and **99.6%** of the high part: highly entangled images
are recognised from the remaining same-class images even without training on them, low-entangled (unusual) images much less.

### 1.7 What Experiment 1 tells us
- **S2 (subset forgetting): highly entangled images are harder to forget.** For every method the forget accuracy after unlearning rises
  from the low to the high part (S2-30: Gradient Ascent 77.7 / 93.2 / 99.2%, GA + Descent 82.7 / 94.9 / 94.8%, Teacher-Student
  64.1 / 93.0 / 98.4%, Random Relabeling 89.5 / 96.9 / 98.9%, Boundary Relabeling 52.1 / 74.6 / 68.7%). The methods that push the medium /
  high parts lower pay with retain and test accuracy (Boundary Relabeling: retain 89.2%, test 82.2% on the high run; Random Relabeling
  S2-10 high run: retain 88.1%); those that keep retain accuracy ~99% leave the high part at 98-99%. This matches the RUM paper's claim
  that entangled forget data cannot be unlearned without hurting the retain data.
- **S1 (single-class forgetting, corrected setup): entanglement does not make forgetting harder.** Every part is forgotten almost
  completely (<= 2.8% forget accuracy, except Random Relabeling + WS on the low part, 17.4%) with retain accuracy 98.8-100%, and the
  forgetting spreads to the unused airplanes. A class is one shared concept: removing it through any third of its images removes it all.

## 2. Experiment 2 - the three parts unlearned one after another (sequential) vs all at once (one-shot)

### 2.1 What runs
For every scenario (4), seed (3), method (5) and with/without the Weight Saliency mask (2), a chain of three runs:
1. **Step 1:** start from the Original model; forget = the low part; retain = everything else (the medium and high parts are still
   treated as retain images in this step).
2. **Step 2:** start from the model produced by step 1; forget = the medium part; retain = everything except the low and medium parts
   (the high part is still retain).
3. **Step 3:** start from the model produced by step 2; forget = the high part; retain = the normal retain set (no forget images left).
4. **Final evaluation:** the model after step 3 is evaluated on the WHOLE forget set and compared with the normal Retrain model of the
   scenario (the one trained without the whole forget set).
That is 4 x 3 x 10 = 120 chains = **360 unlearning runs + 120 final evaluations**. No new Retrain models are needed, because at the end the
whole forget set is removed, exactly as in the normal setting.

**One-shot** = the same method and configuration applied once to the whole forget set (these runs already existed from the main grid /
improvement stages). Experiment 2 asks: does unlearning in the order "least entangled first, most entangled last" (the order proposed
by the RUM paper) end closer to retraining than doing everything at once?

What happened, method by method (S1):
- **Gradient Ascent:** forgets each part in its step (forget accuracy 0.47%, 0.47%, 0.02%) and ends at forget 0.53%, but the three rounds of
  ascent cost more retain/test accuracy than one round (test 82.4% vs 83.2%). Sequential gap 1.34 vs one-shot 1.07: slightly worse.
- **GA + Descent:** in steps 1 and 2 the retain set still contains the other airplanes, so it only partly forgets (51%, 26%); step 3 removes
  the rest (0%). The extra steps cost retain accuracy (95.4% vs 99.1%) and test accuracy (79.0% vs 82.7%). Gap 3.04 vs 1.34: worse.
- **Teacher-Student:** steps 1 and 2 forget almost nothing (98.9%, 93.1%) because the remaining airplanes are taught back; in step 3 the
  model collapses: retain accuracy 34.1%, test 30.7%. Reason: every step contains two "push away from the teacher" epochs, so the chain
  pushes 6 times in total, and we had already seen that more than ~2-4 push epochs make Teacher-Student collapse. Gap 30.43 vs 0.60.
  With the Weight Saliency mask (only half of the weights can move) the chain stays stable: gap 0.98.
- **Random Relabeling and Boundary Relabeling:** steps 1 and 2 forget nothing (100%), because the retain step re-teaches airplanes from the
  airplanes still in the retain set. (Random Relabeling has a retain term in its definition - Weight Saliency paper eq. 6 and its code;
  the Boundary Relabeling paper uses the forget set only, and C1-mix is our variant with an added retain pass. C0, the paper version
  without a retain pass, reached avg_gap 6.92 in S1 one-shot.) In step 3 (no airplanes left in the retain set) they should forget, but they do not (96.1% and 91.8%
  forget accuracy on the high part; 97.9% and 95.6% on the whole forget set). Reason: the forget pass of step 3 contains only 1500 images
  (6 batches per epoch instead of 18 in one-shot), while the retain pass is unchanged (158 batches per epoch), so the forgetting signal is
  three times weaker than in the one-shot run. Gaps 28.65 and 27.91 vs 1.19 and 0.16.
- **Conclusion S1:** sequential unlearning is worse than one-shot for every method in single-class forgetting.

| Random Relabeling | 81.1 | 93.3 | 87.4 | 51.8 | 5.43 | 92.8 | 98.9 | 91.6 | 53.6 | 2.55 |
| Boundary Relabeling | 75.1 | 95.6 | 85.0 | 59.9 | 8.98 | 61.2 | 93.0 | 81.5 | 57.3 | 13.33 |

- **S2-10:** sequential is better for Teacher-Student (2.78 vs 3.81; it does not collapse here because each part has only 450 images) and
  for Random Relabeling (2.98 vs 3.91); worse for Gradient Ascent (5.07 vs 4.80), GA + Descent (4.89 vs 3.52) and Boundary (13.51 vs 11.22).
- **S2-50:** sequential is much better for Random Relabeling (2.55 vs 5.43); Teacher-Student collapses again (61.64); worse for Gradient
  Ascent (6.53 vs 4.18), GA + Descent (9.27 vs 3.80) and Boundary (13.33 vs 8.98).

### 2.5 What Experiment 2 tells us
- The order "least entangled first" helps **Random Relabeling in every subset scenario** (and Teacher-Student in S2-10), as the RUM paper
  suggests for its relabeling-type methods.
- Methods that work by repeated gradient ascent (Gradient Ascent, GA + Descent, and Boundary Relabeling's relabel-and-push) accumulate damage
  over the three steps and are always better done in one shot.
- Teacher-Student cannot be chained without collapsing (except on small forget sets or with the Weight Saliency mask in S1).
- In single-class forgetting, splitting the class into steps does not work for methods with a retain step: as long as part of the class is
  in the retain set, they re-learn it.

## 3. With the Weight Saliency mask
The same experiments were run with the mask (tables in `records/results/entanglement_experiments.md`). In Experiment 1 the gaps change by
less than 0.5 in most cases (larger changes: Teacher-Student S2-30 low part 7.62 -> 3.79; Random Relabeling S2-10 high part 7.78 -> 4.81;
S1 Random Relabeling low part 2.06 -> 6.75, forget accuracy 2.8% -> 17.4%).

(S2-30 6.83 -> 4.89, S2-50 9.27 -> 6.26), but does not prevent the Teacher-Student collapse in S2-30 / S2-50.
